# What to do when you get stains when out on the town
Stains happen and there is nothing worse than spilling red wine or a sauce that is bright yellow from tumeric on a white top. Resist the temptation to reach for the bubbly water or to run to the bathroom and use hand soap. Both are recipes for disaster. Bubbly (sparkling, club or seltzer) water does not have magic properties and hand soap is meant for hands and not fabric.

We at Jeeves suggest using a clean absorbent cloth to blot (not rub) the stained area. Adding a bit of water (tap is fine) will help, but only a splash. Most food or beverage stains are not going to come out if you just blot and use a bit of water, so you may have to suffer with the stain for the evening.
Please resist the temptation to use Google and attempt stain removal on the fly. Dull knives, hair spray, vodka, white wine, and salt applied to the stain are not going to help.
One thing to carry is a small spray bottle of a stain remover such as Chateau Spill or Miss Mouth’s Messy Eater which may lessen the severity of the stain until you can seek professional advice. Both of these products have been tested by Jeeves and they do help. Of course read the instructions on the bottle as they could harm certain fabrics such as silk, suede or leather.
If the garment that is stained is washable, pre-treat the stain and wash as soon as possible. Any garment that is “dry clean only” (check the care label) should be brought to Jeeves for professional care.
Did you try to remove the stain yourself? Please fess up and tell us the dirty details. Our staff can do a much better job if we know exactly what the stain is and how you tried to remove it. We won’t judge, we promise.
Thanks for listening and we hope this helps. Jeeves is always at your service.
